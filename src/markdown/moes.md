## Overview

Partnered with Moe’s Southwest Grill, a popular fast-casual southwestern restaurant in the U.S., our team of 4 researchers and designers explored how voice interaction technology could be implemented into the Moe’s environment. After a long research process which consisted of market research, in-store observations, customer interviews, and customer surveys, our team found that our user-base was resoundingly disinterested in and inexperienced with voice interaction technology. After presenting these findings to our industry partner, we conducted another round of rapid research in the form of a participatory design session to gauge how our user-base would engage with different forms of voice interaction technology in a Moe’s context. We ultimately gathered enough information to design and develop a proof-of-concept voice ordering tool that incorporated all of our research findings. We ultimately used this prototype to demonstrate many of the challenges and nuances that a Moe’s voice ordering tool would entail.

## Market Research

Our initial task was to explore the broad question, how might Moe’s utilize voice interaction technology? Before we could begin to answer that question, our team conducted initial research into the state of voice interaction technology and how it is trending in markets today. We found that voice interaction technology is one of the fastest growing consumer technology spaces. Looking specifically at voice interaction technology in the restaurant business, we found several companies that are utilizing the technology for voice ordering with mild success at best.

## In-Store Observation

To immerse ourselves in the context of our project, we conducted 3 separate hour-long observation sessions in different Moe’s stores in the Atlanta area. The goal of this method was to obtain broad information about the Moe’s in-store experience, identify pain points, and take a detailed look at the ordering process.

## Customer Survey

We conducted an online customer survey with two main goals: gather potential interview participants and collect information about customers’ attitudes towards voice interaction technology. Our primary goal was to gather interview participants so we included only a few questions related to voice interaction technology, but the results we obtained were very telling. Very few Moe’s customers used voice interaction technology and even fewer liked voice interaction technology.

## Interviews

After obtaining participants through the survey, we conducted several semi-structured interviews with customers. We made an effort to conduct several interviews with customers who had used voice interaction technology before and several interviews with customers who hadn’t. We also conducted two interviews with former Moe’s employees to gather a behind-the-scenes perspective on the process of fulfilling orders.

## Research Findings

Bases on the data we gathered from the surveys and interviews, we were able to develop a list of actionable research findings. These included items like “There is often confusion around the Moe’s menu items” and “Some customers are skeptical to trust voice assistant technology.” We then chose to represent our findings in the form of 3 different personas. At this point in the process, our research findings indicated that our users had overwhelmingly negative and skeptical attitudes towards voice interaction technology. On top of this, our data from Moe’s employees pointed towards many nuances and complications in the ordering process. We presented our synthesized findings along with the personas we created to our industry partner.

## Industry Feedback

Our presentation to our Moe’s industry partner painted a clear picture of the mixed and mainly negative attitudes their customers expressed towards voice interaction technology. The partner completely agreed with our assessment and introduced a slight pivot. She introduced the complete rebranding of the Moe’s restaurants to us and invited us to visit the new restaurant that was piloting the new brand. Our team saw this as an opportunity to utilize this new environment to gather ideas from Moe’s customers about how voice interaction might fit into the new brand. Together with our industry partner, we decided to go forward with a two-pronged focus: conduct research specifically targeting the new Moe’s environment and prototype a voice ordering technology based on all of the information we had collected thus far plus information gathered about the new environment.

## Participatory Design

We needed a rapid understanding of how users viewed and interacted with the new Moe’s experience and needed a framework for the voice interaction prototype embodying that space that we would be creating for our industry partners. A participatory design session was a logical choice involving users in a contained exposure to the to the new space and actively working together to envision a prototype or parameters for a prototype that could eventually be developed. As a team we designed an almost 3-hour session that would bring in 6 Moe’s customers and pit them in situations where they could creatively design and react to voice interaction technologies.

## Affinity Mapping

Now that we had finally finished conducting as much user research as we could, we analyzed all of our data using a massive affinity map. This process allowed us to identify patterns in the data from across all of our research methods and develop a useful set of design implications for our voice ordering prototype.

## Voice Ordering Prototype

The final prototype was built as a low-fidelity Google Skill that allows customers to go through the Moe’s ordering process completely through voice interaction. Though there are many technical limitations of our prototype, we incorporated several of the intricacies we discovered through our research such as protein specifications and cheese specifications. The primary goal of the prototype was to demonstrate a proof-of-concept for Moe’s voice ordering, while also demonstrating to our Moe’s partners the intricacies and technical barriers that implementing a voice ordering tool would entail. The prototype was delivered along with an actionable set of guidelines to consider if Moe’s chooses to further implement voice ordering in the future.
