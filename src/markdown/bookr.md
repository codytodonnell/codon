## Overview

Over the course of three and a half months, our team of four sought out to make the gig-booking process easier for local and amateur musicians. We carried out an intensive user-centered design process which consisted of direct research with users, iterative design prototyping, final prototype development, and usability testing. We employed multiple methods of research to understand our user-base which ultimately lead to our final design: Bookr. Bookr connects musicians to gig opportunities and vice versa by matching the two based on common interest in each other.

## Market Research

To better understand the problem space, we conducted initial market research to see what types of existing tools were out there already to help musicians find and book gigs. Two of the most prominent and relevant tools we found were GigTown and Sonicbids. GigTown is designed to be a networking platform that connects musicians, venues, and fans while Sonicbids is designed to be a band-management assistant tool. Both platforms had strengths that we took note of such as simple music sharing, venue details, and musician profiles, but we also noted several weaknesses like the lack of any form of smart recommendation and poor communication features.

## Interviews

Early on in the process we conducted three interviews with musicians based in the U.S. We chose to conduct interviews so that we could gather detailed information about the problem space and dive deep into issues that users were most focused on. For each interview, the interviewer followed a semi-structured format and took detailed notes. After each session, the whole design team convened for an interpretation session where we translated the content of the interview into succinct affinity notes. The interviews and their subsequent analysis yielded key insights into our user-base such as the fact that scheduling gigs with venues is a huge pain point for musicians and that musicians are frequently booking gigs on-the-go. Based on our interviews we were also able to develop a detailed task analysis of the gig-booking process.

## Survey

After collecting detailed qualitative information from interviews with musicians, we decided to craft an online survey to reach a broader range of musicians and collect quantitative evidence of musicians’ pain points and attitudes. We circulated our survey through several online communities as well as through connections of our own which resulted in over 60 responses from across the U.S. Based on data from the survey results we pulled out several key insights that would be critical to our eventual design. One such insight was that finding opportunities is the most difficult part of the gig-booking process and another was that effective communication with the venue is critically important to arranging a gig.

## Divergent Designs

After synthesizing data from both our interviews and online survey into a list of actionable design implications, our team collectively participated in a brainstorming session to generate divergent design solutions to the problems musicians face when booking gigs. We spent 15 minutes rapidly and silently producing three ideas each. Afterwards, each team member clearly presented their ideas and sketches. From those 12 ideas, we identified the three solutions that best addressed musicians and the problems detailed in the data. These solutions were Gig Explorer, Tour Builder, and Bookr. We broke off and developed low-fidelity wireframes for each of those three solutions to help analyze which one would ultimately best solve the problem.

## Prototype Interviews

With three fleshed out wireframe prototypes, we decided to reach back out to two users to hold a prototype interview to collect direct user feedback on the solutions. While each solution received positive feedback from both users, Bookr received the most enthusiastic responses and was lauded for its significant simplification of musician-venue communication and its ability to remove barriers to gig-booking.

## Design Solution

Based both on the results of our prototype interviews and our personal assessment of the data we had gathered up to this point, our team chose Bookr as our design solution. We fleshed out the features and details of the design into an interactive Adobe XD prototype. At a high level, here’s how Bookr works: people seeking musicians post openings for their gig and musicians browse through gigs that fit their preferences. Musicians navigate through a feed of potential opportunities, denoting which ones they are interested in and which ones they are not, with a familiar "swipe to like" interface. Once a musician expresses interest in a gig, the gig-poster will see this ‘match’ on their end. From there, the gig-poster can listen to the interested musician’s music sample and either remove the musician from their booking options, keep them there to decide later, or initiate a chat to get the ball rolling on setting up that performance. At this point the musician has expressed interest in the opportunity, the gig-poster has expressed interest in the musician, and the date and location have already been determined.

## Usability Tests

Using our final prototype, we conducted four moderated task-based user tests. We gave the users two separate tasks to complete, collected video recordings of their usage, administered a short post-test survey, and measured their experience for 11 different usability criteria. Our criteria included metrics such as “Users were able to sign up without any errors” and "Users were at least somewhat satisfied with the musician-type options." These user tests proved to be incredibly helpful not just for validating where our design succeeded, but also where our design needed to be improved. After conducting the tests we documented many actionable implications and changes such as, "Add more options to the music samples page" and "Users should be able to access clarification information for complicated forms."
